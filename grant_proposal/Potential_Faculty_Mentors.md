## How to Research Faculty

### Steps to Identify Best Matches:

1. **Check Department Websites**
   - Review faculty profiles for research interests
   - Look for keywords: "smart cities," "civic tech," "applied AI," "transportation"

2. **Review Recent Publications**
   - Google Scholar search: "University of Rhode Island" + "smart cities" OR "traffic" OR "municipal"
   - Look for applied research rather than purely theoretical

3. **Check Course Catalogs**
   - Faculty teaching relevant courses are often good mentors
   - Courses to look for: Capstone, Senior Design, Applied AI, Web Systems

4. **Ask Current Professors**
   - Your current CS professors may recommend colleagues
   - Department advisors often know faculty interests well

## Approaching Faculty - Best Practices

### Do:
- Research their work before contacting
- Mention specific aspects of their research that relate
- Be clear about time commitment (weekly/bi-weekly meetings)
- Offer to work around their schedule
- Prepare for the meeting (have demo ready)

### Don't:
- Mass email multiple faculty with generic message
- Assume they'll say yes immediately
- Wait until last minute before deadline
- Forget to follow up if no response

## Alternative Mentorship Models

If no single faculty member is available:

1. **Co-mentorship**: Two faculty share mentorship (e.g., CS + Engineering)
2. **Adjunct/Lecturer**: Often more available than tenure-track faculty
3. **Research Staff**: Senior PhD students or postdocs sometimes can co-mentor
4. **External + Internal**: Industry professional + URI faculty member

## Red Flags to Avoid

- Faculty who want to completely change your project direction
- Those who seem too busy to provide regular guidance
- Anyone who doesn't acknowledge your original contribution
- Faculty who don't respond to initial communications

## Success Indicators

Good mentor candidates will:
- Show genuine interest in the civic impact
- Ask thoughtful questions about your approach
- Suggest improvements without taking over
- Have time for regular meetings
- See publication/conference potential

Remember: The best mentor isn't necessarily the most famous professor, but one who is genuinely interested in your success and has time to guide you.
